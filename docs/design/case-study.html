How I built this site

My 2023 portfolio was a hero, an About section and a CV that leaked my phone number. This is how I rebuilt it to feel like meeting me, in four languages, with every decision written down.

Role
Design + frontend
Timeline
Nov 2026 — ongoing
Stack
Next.js 16 · Tailwind 4 · next-intl
Languages
EN · PT · IT · DE
01 · The problem

It didn’t sound like me

The old site was a template with my name on it. No projects, no story, a dark-mode button that did nothing, and a public CV with my birth date, phone and home address.

02 · Constraints

Four languages, 30-second readers

  • Recruiters skim: role, experience and CV must be visible right away
  • EN, PT, IT and DE, and I speak two of them at A1
  • Free hosting and a free Figma plan (one mode, three pages, a tool-call quota)
  • Personal data never goes in the repo again
03 · Options

Three ways forward

Patch v1

Fast, but keeps every structural bug.

Astro

Great for content, less overlap with my day job.

Chosen

Next.js 16

Same stack as work, RSC and i18n routing. Room to grow.

04 · Decision

Rebuild, and write it all down

Every choice gets a log entry: context, options, decision, trade-offs. This page is generated from that log.docs/case-study/decisions.md
05 · Outcome

What changed

4languages from day one
0personal data in the repo
≥95Lighthouse, every category (target)
06 · What I learned

Constraints are design input

Figma’s one-mode limit pushed me to name tokens exactly like the CSS variables they become. When the Figma quota ran out, prototyping in HTML got me closer to the real thing, faster. Writing decisions down made them quicker, not slower, and turned into this page for free.
